Does Your Pet Grooming Business Need a Website? (Yes, and Here Is Why)

KalnyesGrowth June 20, 2026 7 min read

The pet grooming industry is worth over $14 billion and growing every year. More people own pets than ever before, and they are spending more on grooming services than any previous generation. But here is the problem: most pet groomers have no website. They rely on a Facebook page, word of mouth, or a listing on Google that they never update.

Meanwhile, thousands of pet owners in your area are searching "dog grooming near me" or "pet groomer near me" every single month. If you do not have a website, those customers are finding your competitors instead of you.

Why a Facebook Page Is Not Enough

A Facebook business page is better than nothing. But it has serious limitations when it comes to attracting new customers and booking appointments.

Facebook controls who sees your posts. Even your own followers only see a fraction of your content. Your page layout is identical to every other business on the platform. And when someone searches Google for "pet grooming near me," Facebook pages rarely show up in the top results. A dedicated website does.

Your website is yours. You control the design, the content, the booking process, and the customer experience. Nobody can change the algorithm and cut your visibility overnight.

What Pet Owners Look for on a Grooming Website

Pet parents are protective. They are not going to hand their dog or cat to just anyone. Before they call or book, they want to see proof that you know what you are doing and that their pet will be safe and comfortable. Your website needs to answer those questions immediately.

1. Service List With Clear Pricing

List every service you offer: bath and brush, full groom, nail trim, teeth cleaning, de-shedding treatments, flea baths, specialty cuts. Include pricing by size (small, medium, large breeds) so customers know what to expect before they call. Transparency builds trust instantly.

2. Before-and-After Photo Gallery

This is the most powerful section on any pet grooming website. Pet owners love seeing transformations. A matted, scruffy dog turned into a clean, styled pup tells a story that no amount of text can match. Take photos of every groom you do. Build a gallery organized by breed or service type. Update it regularly so returning visitors always see something new.

3. Online Booking

Most pet owners prefer booking online rather than calling. An online booking system lets customers choose a service, pick a date and time, and confirm their appointment in under two minutes. No phone tag. No waiting for business hours. No lost leads. Online booking is the single biggest conversion driver for grooming businesses.

4. Staff and Groomer Profiles

Pet owners want to know who will be handling their animal. Include short bios for each groomer with their photo, experience, and any certifications. If someone is Fear Free certified, trained in specific breed cuts, or has a specialty like cat grooming, highlight that. These details separate you from the competition.

5. FAQ Section

First-time customers have a lot of questions. Address the common ones right on your website:

  • What vaccinations does my pet need before a grooming appointment?
  • How long does a full groom take?
  • Do you groom cats?
  • What if my dog is aggressive or anxious?
  • Do you offer pickup and delivery?
  • What products do you use?

Answering these questions on your site saves you time on the phone and makes customers feel more comfortable booking.

6. Google Reviews Integration

Embed your Google reviews directly on your website. Real reviews from real pet owners are the most persuasive content you can display. If someone is comparing two groomers and one has 80 five-star reviews visible on their homepage, that groomer wins every time.

Mobile Pet Grooming: Special Website Needs

If you run a mobile grooming business, your website needs a few extra features that brick-and-mortar shops do not.

  • Service area map: Show exactly which zip codes or neighborhoods you cover. A simple embedded map with your service zone highlighted tells customers instantly whether you come to them.
  • Van and setup photos: Mobile grooming customers want to see your vehicle and equipment. Post high-quality photos of your van interior, your grooming station, and your tools. A clean, organized setup builds confidence.
  • Booking by location: Let customers enter their zip code or address during booking so they can confirm you serve their area before they fill out the rest of the form.

Trust Signals That Matter

Pet parents are handing you their family member. They need to feel confident that their pet is in safe hands. Here are the trust signals that make the biggest difference:

  • Certifications: National Dog Groomers Association of America (NDGAA), International Professional Groomers (IPG), Fear Free certification, or any breed-specific training.
  • Insurance: Mention that you carry liability insurance. This reassures customers that you take your business seriously.
  • Facility photos: Show your grooming area. Clean floors, organized stations, proper drainage, and good lighting tell customers everything they need to know about your standards.
  • Years in business: If you have been grooming for 5, 10, or 15 years, say so. Experience is a major trust factor in pet services.

Why Design Matters for Pet Businesses

Pet owners are emotionally invested in their animals. The look and feel of your website communicates whether you share that emotional investment. A clean, warm, professional design tells visitors that you take pride in your work. A cluttered, outdated page with blurry photos tells them the opposite.

Your website does not need to be fancy. It needs to be clean, fast, and easy to navigate on a phone. Over 70% of pet owners searching for grooming services are doing it from their mobile device. If your website does not load fast and look good on a small screen, those visitors are leaving within seconds.

The Cost of Not Having a Website

Consider the math. The average full groom costs $50 to $90 depending on your area and the breed. If your website brings in just 3 new customers per week that you would not have reached otherwise, that is $150 to $270 per week in new revenue. Over a year, that adds up to $7,800 to $14,000 in additional income from customers who found you through Google instead of driving past your shop or hearing about you from a friend.

Most of those customers become repeat clients. A single new customer who books monthly is worth $600 to $1,080 per year to your business. Three new customers a week means dozens of repeat clients over the course of a year.

Getting Started

Building a pet grooming website does not have to be complicated or expensive. The process looks like this:

  1. Gather your service list, pricing, and best grooming photos
  2. Write short bios for each groomer on your team
  3. Choose a web design partner who understands service businesses
  4. Set up online booking connected to your calendar
  5. Claim and optimize your Google Business Profile so your website shows up in local search

Most grooming businesses can go from zero online presence to a fully functional, professional website within a week. The sooner you start, the sooner new customers can find you.
